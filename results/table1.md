**Table 1.** Intercoder reliability (two coders).

| Judgment | Units | *n* | Krippendorff's α | 95% CI | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Segmentation: recommendation or not | candidate text blocks | 182 | 0.807 | [0.706, 0.898] | 92.3% |
| Type: machine-specific accommodation vs. general communication | recommendations | 156 | 0.872 | [0.785, 0.945] | 94.2% |
| Type: thinking instructions / emphasis and continuation / persona and motivation / general communication | recommendations | 156 | 0.873 | [0.791, 0.943] | 93.6% |
| Stance: do vs. avoid | recommendations | 156 | 0.954 | [0.879, 1.000] | 98.7% |
| Reason for withdrawal | withdrawals coded by both coders | 81 | 0.876 | [0.781, 0.950] | 91.4% |

*Note.* Nominal α. 95% CIs are percentile intervals from 5000 bootstrap resamples of units (seed 20261111). Segmentation was tested on three documents not used in coder training. Type and stance were double-coded on a 20% sample of recommendations stratified by vendor, with candidate machine-specific accommodations oversampled; recommendations either coder judged out of scope are excluded. A third form of withdrawal, walk-back (a rewritten recommendation that softens its earlier claim), was double-coded but is not analyzed because reliability was low (α = 0.36, 95% CI [0.14, 0.58], n = 138).
