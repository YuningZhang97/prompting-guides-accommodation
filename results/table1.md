**Table 1.** Intercoder reliability (two coders).

| Judgment | Units | *n* | Krippendorff's α | 95% CI | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Segmentation: recommendation or not | candidate text blocks | 182 | .807 | [.706, .898] | 92.3% |
| Type: machine-specific accommodation vs. general communication | recommendations | 156 | .872 | [.785, .945] | 94.2% |
| Type: thinking instructions / emphasis and continuation / persona and motivation / general communication | recommendations | 156 | .873 | [.791, .943] | 93.6% |
| Stance: do vs. avoid | recommendations | 156 | .954 | [.879, 1.000] | 98.7% |
| Reason for withdrawal | withdrawals coded by both coders | 81 | .876 | [.781, .950] | 91.4% |

*Note.* Nominal α. 95% CIs are percentile intervals from 5,000 bootstrap resamples. Segmentation was tested on three documents not used in coder training. The sample for type and stance oversampled candidate machine-specific accommodations and excludes recommendations that either coder judged out of scope. Walk-backs, rewritten recommendations that soften an earlier claim, were also double-coded but are not analyzed because reliability was low (α = .36).
